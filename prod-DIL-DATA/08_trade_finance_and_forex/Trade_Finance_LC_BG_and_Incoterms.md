# Trade Finance & International Banking: Letters of Credit, Bank Guarantees & FEMA

**International Rules:** ICC Uniform Customs and Practice for Documentary Credits (UCP 600)  
**Domestic Statutory Framework:** Foreign Exchange Management Act (FEMA), 1999 & RBI Master Directions on External Trade  

---

## 1. Letters of Credit (LC) Architecture

A Letter of Credit is an irrevocable undertaking issued by an issuing bank upon the instruction of an applicant (buyer) to pay a beneficiary (seller) upon presentation of strictly compliant shipping documents.

### 1.1 Parties to an LC
1. **Applicant (Buyer):** Opens the credit facility.
2. **Issuing Bank (Buyer's Bank):** Undertakes the primary, independent payment obligation.
3. **Advising Bank:** Authenticates the letter of credit and forwards it to the seller.
4. **Confirming Bank (Optional):** Adds its own independent payment guarantee, mitigating buyer country political risk.
5. **Beneficiary (Seller / Exporter):** Entitled to payment upon presentation of compliant documents.

### 1.2 Core Operational Rules under UCP 600
- **The Doctrine of Strict Compliance (Article 14):** Documents presented must appear on their face to constitute a compliant presentation. The standard is strict document verification (Bill of Lading, Commercial Invoice, Certificate of Origin, Insurance Policy).
- **Independence Principle (Article 4):** A letter of credit is by its nature a separate transaction from the sale contract on which it may be based. Banks are in no way concerned with or bound by such contracts, even if any reference to it is included in the credit.
- **Examination Period (Article 14b):** The issuing bank has a maximum of **five banking days** following the day of presentation to determine whether the presentation is compliant or to notify discrepancies.

## 2. Bank Guarantees (BG): Financial vs. Performance

A Bank Guarantee is an irrevocable commitment by a bank to pay a specified sum if the customer fails to fulfill contractual or financial commitments.

| Feature | Financial Guarantee | Performance Guarantee |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Obligation Nature** | Guarantees financial payment (e.g., in lieu of cash deposit, earnest money, advance payment) | Guarantees technical completion or operational performance of a project/contract |
| **RBI Risk Weight** | **100% Risk Weight** (equivalent to credit advance) | **50% Risk Weight** |
| **Margin Requirement** | Typically 10% to 25% cash margin / FD lien | Typically 10% to 20% cash margin + counter-indemnity |
| **Invocation / Claim** | Payable immediately on first demand without protest | Payable on notice of contractual failure or default |

### Unconditional & Irrevocable Nature
Under Indian Contract Act Section 126 and settled Supreme Court jurisprudence, courts will not grant injunctions against the encashment of an unconditional bank guarantee unless clear **fraud of an egregious nature** or **irretrievable injustice** is proven.

## 3. FEMA Guidelines on Import / Export Settlements

- **Export Realization Period:** Full value of exported goods/services must be realized and repatriated to India within **nine months** from the date of export (or 15 months for goods exported to overseas warehouses).
- **Import Payment Timeline:** Settlement for imported goods must be executed within **six months** from shipment date (unless deferred settlement is approved under capital goods credit).
